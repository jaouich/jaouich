[Slide 1 · 12.8s]
Lesson two. A prompt is simply the instruction you give to an AI. A better prompt gives a better answer. Here is a simple formula you can use every day.

[Slide 2 · 11.6s]
Look at this prompt: write about marketing. It is too vague. The AI has to guess everything, so the answer will be generic and not very useful.

[Slide 3 · 18.4s]
Use five parts. One, the role: who should the AI be. Two, the task: what exactly should it do. Three, the context: who is the answer for. Four, the format: a list, a table or an email. Five, the limits: length, tone and level.

[Slide 4 · 15.2s]
A good prompt looks like this. Act as a friendly teacher. Explain what an online portfolio is, for a beginner with no experience. Use five bullet points, in simple English, under one hundred and twenty words.

[Slide 5 · 15.6s]
Do not stop at the first answer. Read it, then say exactly what to change. Ask for another version, or ask the AI what it is unsure about. A conversation gets better results than a single question.

[Slide 6 · 12.4s]
Examples help a lot. Paste a short sample of the style you like, show the result you want, and say what to avoid. The AI follows examples very well.

[Slide 7 · 13.6s]
Remember: a clear prompt gives a useful answer. Practice the five-part formula today with one real task. In the next lesson we will learn how to check what the AI tells you.
