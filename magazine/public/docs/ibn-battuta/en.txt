[Slide 1 · 12s]
Welcome to Jerry Documentaries. In this episode we follow Ibn Battuta, a young man from Tangier who started a pilgrimage and ended up traveling for almost thirty years.

[Slide 2 · 16.8s]
Ibn Battuta was born in Tangier, in Morocco, in the year thirteen hundred and four, into a family of scholars. He studied Islamic law and grew up in a city that was a meeting place of sailors, merchants and ideas.

[Slide 3 · 14.8s]
In thirteen twenty five, at about twenty one years old, he left Tangier to make the pilgrimage to Mecca. He crossed North Africa, sometimes alone and sometimes with caravans, and reached Egypt, Syria and Arabia.

[Slide 4 · 15.2s]
After the pilgrimage he kept going. He visited Persia and Iraq, then Anatolia and Central Asia, and arrived in India, where he spent several years serving at the court of a sultan before continuing his way.

[Slide 5 · 17.2s]
He sailed to the Maldives and Southeast Asia, and according to his account he reached China. He also traveled by sea along the coast of East Africa. Some historians discuss parts of his story, but his main route is well known.

[Slide 6 · 14.8s]
Historians estimate that he traveled around one hundred and twenty thousand kilometers in almost thirty years, much more than famous European travelers of his time, and he visited the lands of dozens of today's countries.

[Slide 7 · 13.6s]
Near the end he traveled again, crossing the Sahara to the kingdom of Mali around thirteen fifty two. Then he returned to Morocco, where he finally settled after decades on the road.

[Slide 8 · 17.2s]
The Sultan of Morocco asked him to tell his story, and a writer named Ibn Juzayy wrote it down. The book, called the Rihla, was finished in thirteen fifty five and describes cities, customs, food and people across the known world.

[Slide 9 · 13.6s]
Ibn Battuta teaches us curiosity, patience and respect for other cultures. One road is rarely enough to understand the world, and every journey begins with a first step out of the door.
