=== VOICE-OVER SCRIPT ===
[Slide 1 · 14s]
Welcome back to Jerry Documentaries. Every day we open pages, send messages and watch videos, but how does the internet really work? Let us follow one click from your phone to the world.

[Slide 2 · 14.8s]
The internet is not one big computer. It is a network of networks: millions of smaller networks of homes, companies, schools and phone operators, all agreeing to talk to each other using the same rules.

[Slide 3 · 15.6s]
First, the address. You type a name like example dot com. A system called DNS works like a phone book: it turns that name into a number, an IP address, that tells computers where the website lives.

[Slide 4 · 14.8s]
Second, packets. Your request and the answer are cut into many small pieces called packets. Each packet can take a different route, and at the end they are put back together in the right order.

[Slide 5 · 14.8s]
Third, the journey. Your phone sends the packets through Wi-Fi or a mobile signal to your provider, then through large cables on land and under the sea. Most international internet traffic travels through undersea cables.

[Slide 6 · 16.8s]
Fourth, the server. A server is simply a computer that is always on and answers requests. It stores web pages, videos and apps, and sends the answer back along the same kind of path in a fraction of a second.

[Slide 7 · 14.4s]
The story began in 1969, when researchers connected computers in the United States on a network called ARPANET and sent the first message between them. It was the seed of what we use today.

[Slide 8 · 17.6s]
The web is different from the internet. The internet is the network. The World Wide Web is a system of pages and links that runs on it, proposed by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989, with the first website appearing in 1991.

[Slide 9 · 14.4s]
A last word about safety. Check the address before you log in, use strong and different passwords, and never share secret recovery phrases or passwords with anyone, even if they say they are support.

[Slide 10 · 10.8s]
